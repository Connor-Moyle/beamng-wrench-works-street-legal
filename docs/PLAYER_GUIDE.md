# Wrench Works: Street Legal - player guide

(Made from the in-game Help, so the two always agree. Open Help on the phone or the laptop to read it in the game.)

## Welcome

Wrench Works: Street Legal is a career on West Coast USA. You start on foot with a few thousand dollars and no car. Buy something that runs, fix it up in the garage, earn money by day, race on the streets by night and build a name.

Your phone (key K) runs everything: races, cars, jobs, the bank, the markets. The same things are on the laptops in the garages.

Benny, who runs your first garage, texts you where to begin. Open Messages.

## Your first hour

- Read Benny's texts and follow the tutorial: it walks you through buying a car.
- Get a car. The Cab app takes you to a dealer or the junkyard, where a few cars stand in a row. Look at them with left and right, test drive one, buy it. The cheapest car on the used lot is always one you can afford.
- Take it to your garage. Drive up to the door: the car backs onto the lift and the garage screen opens. Click a part to take it off, click a shelf part to fit it. Fixing a bent panel costs money, so look before you buy.
- Earn some money with a job from the Jobs app (by day), then spend it on parts from the Part market.
- When you are ready, start the night and race.

## The phone

The home screen has the apps: Races, My cars, Bank, Street cred, Jobs, Wait, Club, Turf, Car market, Part market, Map, Camera, Gallery and Help. Messages, Contacts, Phone and Cab are in the dock.

The Map lists the places by kind with their distance; tap one and the route shows on the road (it clears when you get there, or with Clear route); Route to my garage on the home screen does the same for your garage. The Camera takes a picture without any screen (also kept in screenshots/street_legal); in the Gallery a photo can be the wallpaper of the phone or the laptop; a new car is photographed at the end of its purchase scene (Settings: Photo of a new car).

Contacts also lists the people you have met (rivals, crew leaders, dealers) with where you met them and a Route to button.

Press K to open and close it. B (or Back) goes one step back. On a pad the d-pad moves and A opens.

## The garage

Drive or walk into a garage door at walking pace. The bar at the top has: Drive out, Catalog, Car info, Mechanic, Tune, Lift, Paint booth, Services, Races, Drivers, Test engine, Settings and Photo.

- Mechanic: click a part to take it off, click a shelf part to fit it. Hover a part in the catalog to see what it does.
- Tune: every tuning item of the car on the right. Changes take hold when you Apply.
- Paint booth: three colours, finishes and mixing sliders. The second and third colours show on trim, bumpers and, on many cars, the rims.
- Services: oil, coolant, battery, nitrous, an engine rebuild. Races and Drivers have their own tabs.
- Photo: hides the screen and saves a picture of the car. Showcase turns the camera round it.

## Cars and markets

The Car market has two tabs: the Dealers (their cars today, with pictures, power and when they arrive) and Private ads (cars sold from a kerb). A car you buy is built at your garage after a few hours; the phone tells you when.

The Part market has Revline (parts from private sellers), Swap & Bid (auctions, and the swap meet), Classifieds (the paper) and the Catalog (new parts from the garage, delivered to your shelf).

Used things can be worse than they look. A seller with few stars, or an ad that says "must go today", is a warning. You can make an offer, pick up in person or pay for delivery.

A private ad for a car tells you its history (previous owners, the service record, accidents) but not its faults. A mechanic's check, for a fee, shows the faults and the car report, and what each fault costs to put right. A patchy service record is a hint. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Used-car checks and the ads show every fault.

Sponsorships: winning in the cars of a maker, or in a car with three or more parts of one aftermarket brand, builds standing with that brand. At 100 standing the brand offers a sponsorship by text: 10% off its parts (15% from 300 standing), a weekly retainer, and a promise to win two or three races a week (a maker also wants a race in its car). Miss the weeks and you get a warning, a strike, and then the deal ends. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Brand sponsorships.

Brand invitationals: a brand you sponsor for four weeks, or whose cars you win in, may text you an invitation (three in a career at most): a race on a long dockyards circuit, in a race car the brand lends you. It is on the Calendar; be at the grid at 15:00 on the day and the car is there for you. No fee. A win pays money, reputation and standing with the brand, and a trophy; a dented car halves the standing. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Brand invitationals.

Crew mates (off by default; Mods > Parts of the game > Crew mates): take a district from a rival crew and one of its people joins you, once per crew. The Riverbed Runners give a mechanic (5 % off parts in the shops, 10 % off service), the Southside Kings a tipster (a text a game day about the next race), North End and Ring Road a scout (calls the police ahead; with the Drivers mod he rides in the passenger seat after the next respawn of your car). A mate is a person only: no car, never in a race.

Seasons (off by default; Mods > Parts of the game > Seasons): the career has four seasons of four weeks, spring first. They change how often it rains or fogs, the air temperature (the rain falls as snow where it is cold enough, with Car Surface FX, and it is colder up high), whether a clear day is hot for thirst, and a few per cent on some purses (winter sprints and rallies pay more, summer shows more, winter shows less). The weather line on the phone names the season and week.

Career travel: from a reputation of 300 (Johnson Valley), 450 (Hirochi) or 600 (Utah) the Calendar offers race series on other maps: the Hirochi circuit cup, the NGRC Utah Rally and the Johnson Valley off-road series. Go there from the event: choose up to three cars (when none of yours fits the series you can take a loaner for a deposit), pay the fee and $200 a car, and you arrive the same evening.

Career travel, on the trip: a round is run a day: Start in the Event app starts the game's own race or rally stage with your car (the Utah stages have pacenotes and the co-driver); the Wait app's next morning sleeps to the next day. The table shows the field; the prize comes after the last round. Go home any time (the rounds you did not run count as missed): the way back is free. If the game stops during a trip, the next start asks whether to go back to the map or go home. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Career travel.

Shops: a themed screen for tyres, exhausts and other shops, with the part brand on each card; a brand sponsorship takes its percentage off the price. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Shops.

Hill duels: at night the three hill roads (Cell Hill, the Alleyway climb, the Mountain loop) carry a marker at their start. The prompt there offers a duel against a rival for a bet: two cars, no finish line, points. The leader scores while 25 m or more ahead, the follower while 12 m or less behind; off the road for more than two seconds costs a point a second. It ends when you finish the road; more points wins, a tie goes to the first across. A win pays the bet and 30 reputation. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Hill duels.

Grab bag: a good week (three jobs, two race wins or $2,000 earned by the weekly ledger) leaves a box of used parts: Benny texts, and the next time you drive a car of yours into the garage two to four parts that fit it go on its shelf for nothing. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Grab bag.

Car theft (off by default): on foot by a parked car the prompt offers a break-in; the car age decides the way in (an old car: the lock and the wires; a 1990s car: a bypass box first; a modern car: a fob tool), noise draws witnesses and the alarm, and a stolen car is hot (maybe tracked) until it is resprayed and on bought plates ($700; $300 for your own cars). Every number is a row on the Car theft page.

Calendar: the Calendar app on the phone lists the weekend races at the Belasco dockyards (days 6 and 7 of the week, 15:00, one of six circuits, an entry fee and a purse), the swap meet nights and the monthly series to come. Enter a race on the phone and be on the grid at the hour: a rival of your standing lines up next to you for three laps. A reminder comes a day and an hour ahead. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Calendar.

Barn finds: from 100 reputation, every few days a contact texts about an old car rotting in a yard far from town. Drive to the spot and the car shows in the Car market as a cheap private ad in a poor state; a mechanic check shows what is wrong with it. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Barn finds.

Servicing: with Servicing and wear on (Mods > Parts of the game), cars wear much faster than before and a service is due about every 150 km; My cars shows each car's sections, the km since the last service and the price. A basic service restarts the interval; a full overhaul also puts the worn sections right. Driving past the interval wears the car faster. Mods > Difficulty > Wear sets the wear rate and with it the service interval (0.5x, 1x, 2x, 4x), and tells Part Wear and the Car Surface FX tyres to wear at the same rate when they are installed.

## Money and the bank

By day you earn money: jobs, the track events, and selling what you do not need. Night pays in reputation instead.

The Bank app shows your balance and statement. You can borrow against your reputation, your garage and the cars you own, up to a limit; a quarter of the loan is due each week, and missed payments cost reputation, then the car.

Insurance costs a little every week and pays half of repair and impound bills over a deductible.

Weekly costs: every seventh day the rent of the garage you hold (the first is free, the second $150, the third $400) and $40 for each car you keep beyond two are paid, and the Bank app lists the coming bills and the last weeks. What you cannot pay becomes a debt at the bank, never a repossession; it costs 5 reputation once, is paid down first from money above $200, and the bank lends nothing while you owe it. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Weekly costs.

The pace: day jobs pay about a third more than they used to. Your first garage is free; the second needs 175 reputation and $6,000, the third 1,000 reputation and $25,000. A few days of jobs and a few night wins get you the second.

## Dealers, factory orders and the car report

At a dealer, walk up to the door marker and choose Look at the cars. The game's own screen elements hide while you look. Left and right change the car; leave the camera alone and it plays cinematic shots (the wheels, the badge, the engine bay with the hood open); touch the camera to stop them.

Every car has a card with Test drive, Buy and Show full car report. The report opens a panel with every part by section (condition, the worst fault, a repair estimate) and scrolls with the wheel, by dragging or with the pad's stick. Close or B shuts it.

High-end dealers also take factory orders: Order from the factory opens a catalogue of every model with its trims. Pick a trim, then the factory options (paint, interior, wheels, steering wheel and more) with a live price. The car arrives at your garage six game hours after you pay, and My cars lists it as on order. Some vehicles (the quad bike, buggy, crawlers, loaders) can only be bought this way.

Dealers, the junkyard and the scrap yard are open by day and say "Closed. Opens at HH:00" at night.

## Weather, wet roads and puddles

Each half of a day has its own weather: clear, cloudy, rain in three strengths, and fog banks at night. The forecast is on the phone's home screen and in the Wait app.

In rain the sky goes grey and dark and the road gets wet. A wet road has less grip, so brake earlier. Puddles collect on the road: a wheel through one throws spray and leaves a wet trail, and at speed a deep puddle drags the car and can make it aquaplane (the wheels float and steering goes light). Lift off and keep straight.

The roads dry out after the rain. Some jobs pay more in bad weather.

## Dirt, scratches and washing

With the optional Car Surface FX mod installed your cars get wet paint in the rain, pick up dust or mud on the road and can be scratched in a knock. Without it nothing here applies.

- My cars (phone and laptop) says how dirty and scratched each car is, for example "Dusty, light scratches". Dirt is clean, dusty or muddy; scratches are none, light or heavy.
- Wash: roll slowly through a car wash bay, or choose Wash at a fuel pump ($15). It takes the dirt off. It does not take scratches off.
- Repair: a repair in the garage (Wrench Works) buffs the scratches out with the dents. A paint job leaves the car clean and unscratched.
- A car you put away keeps its dirt and scratches, and so does the save.

## Car meets

At night a car meet fills a car park with parked cars in the painted bays, each with its owner beside it and some owners in pairs and trios. The meet has a theme (JDM, muscle, drift or euro) and a purple marker on the map.

Walk up to an owner on foot and press the prompt (Talk to the owner). They tell you a story and make an offer: a tip, a race word or a car for sale. Nothing opens by itself while you walk through. The music is loudest at one car and the crowd murmur is in the middle.

At high heat (4 or 5) the police can raid a meet: you get a text, the cars and people scatter and the heat goes up a little. Keep your heat low near a meet.

## Style shows

On Saturday nights, from nine, there is a Style show at one of the meet spots. Drive your car to the marker and stand on it: the entry is $60. Judges score the car on paint and finish, wheels, aftermarket parts (a full set from one maker scores best), cleanliness, body damage and wear, against a field of rival cars. The top three win a purse and reputation (up to $1500 at the top of the ladder).

After the judging the result card says what the judges thought of each part of the car and what to put right first: wash the car, repair the body, respray it, fit matching parts, replace the worn ones. If a mod the judges look at is not installed, that part of the score is left out and the others count for more.

- Style shows (Mods > Street Legal > Parts of the game): switch the shows off if you do not want them.

## Jobs

The Jobs app lists what is on offer now. Taking one gives you a destination or a task, and it pays when you finish: couriers, taxi, parts runs, test drives, dyno and quarter-mile jobs and more. Most jobs are by day; a few shady ones are by night.

Some jobs need a car you own, some a certain reputation. Finish the job you are on before you take another.

Contracts: from 100 reputation, a few part runs and courier parcels carry terms (no damage, within 80% of the time limit, delivered while the road is wet, or in a car worth $15,000 or more). Keep the terms and the job pays 1.6 to 2.2 times as much and gives 5 extra reputation; miss them and you get the normal pay. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Contracts.

Speed traps: six traps and two zones sit on long straight roads (the Speed traps app lists them with a target and your best). Drive through a trap and your speed counts; drive between the two gates of a zone and your average speed counts. The first time you beat a target it pays $250 (a trap) or $400 (a zone) and gives 5 reputation. Danger zones: the longest jump you fly pays $150, $300, $600 and $1,000 at 20, 40, 70 and 110 m, once each, and two drift zones score your drift points against a target of 600 ($300). Both can be switched off in Mods > Parts of the game.

Car mastery: every car you own earns levels by the kilometres you drive it and the races you finish without a wreck (a fifth of a point per kilometre, 20 per clean race; 100, 300, 600, 1000 and 1600 points are levels 1 to 5). Each level takes 2% off its insurance while you drive it, 2% off its service and the tyre shops, and adds 1.5% when you sell it privately. The level shows next to the car on My cars; it goes with the car when it is sold. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Car mastery.

Tier perks: each boss you beat gives a lasting advantage, listed on the Street cred page: cheaper garage upgrades and services, better pay on jobs, lower police fines and a higher loan limit. Switch it off in Mods > Parts of the game > Tier perks.

Taxi fares: a walker waits at the kerb. Drive smoothly; hard braking and hard cornering cost the tip (up to half the fare). Tow jobs: a dead car waits at the kerb; hook it to your car with the tow coupler of the game and tow it to the garage; the clock and the damage are the towed car itself. Parts runs from the scrapyard carry fragile parts. Dealer deliveries take a new car to the customer; with Car Surface FX a single new scratch means no pay.

## Day and night

The career has two halves. Day (07:00 to 19:00) is the legal half: jobs, shops and dealers, the track events with prize money. Night (21:00 to 04:00) is the street half: street races, meets, pink slips. Shops and dealers close at night and open again in the day.

The clock runs slowly inside each half and stops at its end. Start the night or Start the day from the Wait app or a garage computer when you are ready. You cannot switch in a race, on a job or with the police on you. A new day starts when you start the day.

The speed of the clock is a setting (Clock speed).

## Street races

Racers are in your Contacts. Call or text one: a race for money, for high stakes, or for pink slips. A pink slip race is for keeps: win and their car is yours, lose and they drive off in yours.

You can also flash your high beams at a racer cruising the streets, or roll up to a car meet at night. Races start at a start line with flares along the route. Reputation from a win is bigger at night and bigger still with heat on.

Races are only at night. At the start line the race starts when the lights go green.

## Heat and the police

Heat is how much the police want you, from 1 to 5. It only exists at night: Start the day and it is gone. A street race, flashing past a patrol and escaping a chase raise it; it fades while nobody is after you.

The hotter it is, the more patrols, the harder they are to shake, and from 4 there are roadblocks. Reputation counts for more: +15% per heat level.

Reputation you earn at night is "at risk" until you bank it by getting back to a garage (or switching to day). If you are busted you lose what is not banked, pay a fine, and at heat 4 or 5 the car is impounded.

## Clubs and track events

Your garage is in a club, and a club runs a season of seven days. Each day has a night event (a head-to-head street race against the racer just above you) and a day event (a track event from the map: a time trial, drag strip or circuit).

Run a day event from its marker on the map: the stars you earn place you (three stars is first). Points make the standings; a good season pays money and reputation and invites you up. Skipped events only cost their points.

The Club app has today's events and the standings.

## Crews and turf

Every racer is in one of four crews, and each crew holds districts of West Coast USA. Beat a crew's racers on a district's streets at night to take it. A district you hold gives you more reputation and less heat there.

A crew that loses a district calls you out to win it back. Hold four districts and the crew leaders come for you one by one in the turf war. The Turf app has a map.

## Swap meet and auctions

On weekend nights a swap meet fills the biggest car park in town with stalls. Go there and buy parts on the spot. It is on the Part market page of the phone and the laptop, and says where it is.

Swap & Bid is the auction side: put a bid on a lot, and it is yours if nobody outbids you before it ends.

Car auctions (Car market, on the phone and the laptop): six used cars with a grade from A to E and the full car report. The reserve stays secret, the screen only says whether it is met. A bid in the last seconds adds time. Win one and it is delivered to your garage; a car can hide a fault the report does not show, and the mechanic tells you after you have it. Put one of your own cars up with a reserve of your choosing. Every seventh day, 10:00 to 13:00, the junkyard holds a live auction: walk up and press Join the car auction.

## Graphics: anti-aliasing and sharpening

Wrench Works adds a group to the game's Options > Graphics page, under the anti-aliasing type: "Wrench Works: anti-aliasing and sharpening". The first time after installing, restart the game once to see it.

- Anti-aliasing: Game's own (the default; nothing changes), Off, FXAA, FXAA HQ, SMAA High, SMAA Ultra, or SMAA Ultra with Temporal smoothing (it blends with the last frame to calm shimmer but adds no detail). Each choice has a note under it, and Game's own puts the game back exactly as it was.
- Sharpening (off by default): AMD CAS, AMD FSR 1 RCAS or NVIDIA NIS, with a strength slider.
- Presets: Balanced, Smooth, Maximum. Heavier choices cost frame time, so try one and watch the performance overlay.

## Optional extras

- Performance overlay: ESC > Mods menu > Street Legal > Developer (off by default) shows the frame time, the video memory and counts of what is in the world. Use it when the game stutters, and say what it showed when you report it.
- Rigged driver: the rigged driver is the default for your own car on the checked models; switch to Props in Mods > Driver model if you see problems. Other cars use the props driver.

## Settings and tools

All settings are in one place: press ESC and open the Mods menu. Each mod has a page with tabs (for Street Legal: Difficulty, Street racing, Clock and weather, Screens and texts, Accessibility, Police chase, Night traffic, Rivals, The climb, Car auctions, Developer); every tab has a Reset to defaults. Nothing is set on the phone or the laptop.

Difficulty has the police and their aggression, pay, hardcore mode (no pull-outs, no insurance, no free cab) and crew raids; Street racing has the race traffic and flares.

- Street Legal dashboard: the game's own HUD is used by default; switch this on for the Street Legal gauges and map.
- Performance overlay: frame time and counts, for reporting stutters.
- Text size (Normal, Large, Huge): applies to the phone, the laptop and the HUD; on a pad the phone is never smaller than Large.
- Colour-blind friendly colours: blue and orange instead of green and red on the phone, the laptop and the HUD, with a tick, a cross or an arrow beside every good or bad figure.
- Show co-driver notes on screen: on rally stages what the co-driver calls is also written on screen.
- Record street races: a race can be watched again (Races app, Last race) and saved as a clip.
- Phone skin (Screens and texts): Classic, or Slate, a modern look; your own skins are folders in the user folder (see docs/PHONE_SKINS.md). A phone that is already open keeps its look until you close and reopen it.
- Parts of the game (Mods > Street Legal > Parts of the game, and the Active features page): spectators, billboards, ambient sounds, puddles, achievements, tips, car and part markets, rally, street races and drag, clubs, crews, the tow truck, the taxi ride, the bank, the hospital, the HUD pill, the laptops, Maps, garage upgrades, jobs, weekly costs, servicing and wear, used-car checks, contracts, tier perks, speed traps, danger zones, barn finds, sponsorships, the calendar, career travel, shops, hill duels, the grab bag, car theft and cinematic moments, and Needs (off by default). Each is on by default (except Needs) and can be switched off; what a part keeps in your save is not deleted.
- Street Legal in free roam (Mods > Street Legal > Free roam, all off by default): a master switch and one switch each for the phone (K) with the Maps and Cab apps, map pins, weather, the free cab, the free car wash and the dashboard. No career is started or saved and no money is spent.
- Wet roads and walls (Mods > Street Legal > Clock and weather, off by default): in the rain the terrain layers get darker and shinier (walls and roofs stay dry). An earlier version lost the graphics device at High graphics in heavy rain on BeamNG 0.40; this one passed one long test, so turn it on only if you accept the risk.
Developer: a button that gives you all the money, for testing.
